**This table measures the registry, not the birds.** Mats are the lake’s main roost structure, yet they hold a small fraction of sightings — because a bird is only assignable to a *named* mat when the mat is identifiable, and only MAT-01 has a durable mark (its log). Most mat birds are `MAT-unassigned`.

Known mat use: cormorants roost on MAT-01 (three dates, matched by the log); a Great Blue Heron on MAT-03; a mute swan **nest** on MAT-04 in April; four cygnets **sitting on** a mat in August. Mats serve for nesting, roosting and loafing, not just roosting.

## Seasonal narrative

- **Winter.** The lake freezes bank to bank (2026-01-31), snow on both shores. No birds found on that visit; whether birds use the ice is an open question Geno expects to answer next winter.
- **March, pre-breeding.** The resident pair together on 03-25 and 03-27. Mallards at the west end. First goose record with data (03-26), plus an unidentified diving duck that is not a mallard and was not resolvable.
- **April, nesting.** Two adults at a nest on floating mat MAT-04 (04-27), one sitting. A goose flock of 10–15 on the water behind them.
- **June, hatching.** Four downy cygnets by 06-13. Cormorants and a mallard using a mat by 06-17. A swan out of the water on mown grass, 06-19.
- **July–August, growth.** Cygnets part-feathered by July, near-grown and still wholly brown-grey through August. The family ranges to the ocean end (08-11, 08-12). Largest goose group of the year, 07-09.
- **September.** Cygnets still with no white by the 20th. Cormorants on MAT-01 across three dates. One Great Blue Heron, probably immature.

**Brood survival: 4 of 4, June to September.** See `logs/individuals.md`.

## Water-health crossover — hypotheses, not findings

§8 suggests cormorant and heron activity indexes fish presence, and goose and gull concentrations index nutrient loading. Stating plainly what this record can and cannot support:

- **Cormorants are regular** (10 sightings, 5 dates) and roost on the mats. Consistent with fish being present. No independent fish data.
- **Geese are abundant** (36 sightings, second species). Under §8 that would index nutrient loading — but effort is uneven and the sample is opportunistic, so this is not an index of anything yet.
- **Nothing can be said about change over time.** One season, uneven effort, a third of the lake unvisited.

**What would make these real:** a fixed transect walked the same way at a comparable time, even monthly. An index needs consistent effort, and this record does not have it. That is the single most valuable change available to the water-quality thread.

## Water quality — the record begins

### The exhaustive sweep, 2026-09-21

All 262 photographs were then swept frame by frame, cropped to the water band.
An earlier reading of ~80 sampled frames had concluded the lake "photographs
clean". **It does not, and the sample missed everything** — it was drawn from
montages built for bird work, so it leaned toward frames containing birds.

| date | finding |
|---|---|
| 2026-03-25 | **dumped drum** — yellow-and-green, ~half a metre, half-submerged at the water's edge. The largest single piece of dumped material in the archive |
| 2026-07-09 | water strongly **ochre-brown** under a clear sky. Also the largest goose count of the year |
| 2026-08-11 | **floating carcass**, ~40 cm, across four frames. A waterbird or a large fish — not resolvable. NOT a swan-family member; all six were counted alive the same day |
| 2026-08-12 | **vivid green floating patches** — clumped, drifting. Most consistent with duckweed or filamentous algae. Small; nothing like a bloom |
| 2026-09-17 | **dead fish** on the dock planking — independently confirms log Entry 2, which recorded it from EXIF-stripped photos |
| 2026-09-21 | **litter and debris** at the western trash racks: 8–14 discrete items, plus an organic mat banded across the lower mesh |

Water read **strongly ochre-brown** on 07-09, 08-15, 09-04, 09-10 and 09-20, and
grey or blue-reflective on 08-12, 01-31 and 09-21.

### What the sweep changed about the method

It **overturned a rule this project had just written**. Colour had been excluded
outright, on the strength of pixel sampling that showed illumination dominating.
That was the wrong test: under a **clear blue sky** a merely reflective surface
reads blue, so water reading ochre-brown under blue sky is genuinely brown.
Colour is now recordable as a **judged category gated on clear sky** — never as
a measurement, and `water.py` enforces the gate.

**Cause remains undeterminable from a photograph.** Sediment, tannin and an
algal tint all look the same. The brown is real; what it is, is not a
photographic question.

### Where the record stands

Eight observations across seven dates, one of them at the fixed station. **This
is a baseline, not a trend.** No statement about change over time is available
and none should be attempted. A single carcass is not a fish kill; §8 lists
abrupt fish kills as a water-health signal and this does not meet it.

What would make this a real series: repeat photographs at WQ-1 on every west-end
walk, and a **Secchi disk** (~$30) — the only instrument that would turn any of
this into measurement.
