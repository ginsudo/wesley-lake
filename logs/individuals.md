# Wesley Lake — Individuals Catalogue

Tier A and Tier B birds only (PROTOCOL.md §6). Append-only.

**There are no Tier A birds.** Across 219 photographs on 30 dates, not one bird
carries a durable individual mark — no band, collar, wing tag, permanent injury,
bill deformity, leucistic patch or entangled tackle has been found. That is not
a gap in the looking; the hard-mark check has been run and has produced clean
negatives (below). It is what this lake's avifauna is like, and it is why
`STATE.md` treats individual identity as mostly unavailable here.

## SWAN-B01 + SWAN-B02 — the resident mute swan pair

**Tier B** — territorial, within-season, inferred from site fidelity and from
being a one-pair species on a small lake. **Not** individually distinguishable
from each other: B01 and B02 are labels for "the pair", and where a single adult
appears it is recorded as B01 by convention, not by identification.

State the inference every time (§6): *same stretch, same pair, assumed.*

### Hard-mark check

Run on every close approach. All negative:

- **2026-09-17, IMG_3402** — a cormorant, not a swan, but the best hard-mark
  frame in the archive: feet fully resolved on bare dock planking, **unbanded**,
  no tags, no deformity, no entanglement. A real negative, not a "not visible".
- **2026-06-17, IMG_2187** — cormorant on a mat, legs resolved at the waterline,
  unbanded.
- **2026-06-19** — mallards on grass, legs resolved, unbanded.
- No swan has yet been photographed with its legs clear of the water at a
  resolution that would show a band. Swan legs are almost always submerged;
  this is the standing gap in the check, not an absence of marks.

### If a mark is ever found

Open a Tier A record here immediately, record the mark verbatim, and note that
past sightings become searchable retroactively through the feature strings in
`analysis/<date>-observations.json` (§6). Do not backfill an identification onto
earlier records — a mark found in 2027 does not tell you which 2026 bird it was.
