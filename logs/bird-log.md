### Entry 5 — BACKFILL of the full photo archive, logged 2026-09-20

**30 dates, 2025-06-10 to 2026-09-19, 219 photographs.** Appended as one block because that is when it was logged; the dates inside run in chronological order. Entries 1–4 above were logged earlier and cover only three of these dates.

**How this was produced.** Every frame was swept by a local YOLOX detector at native resolution (`pipeline/detect.py`), then every detection reviewed by Claude as a native-resolution crop. Counts are **maximum-simultaneous within a sighting event** — never summed across frames, because re-photographing inflates a naive total about 2.8x. Segments are derived from longitude against the three real crossings. Full records with per-detection provenance: `analysis/<date>-observations.json`.

**Species IDs are Claude’s read unless a line says CONFIRMED.** Geno confirmed four firsts on 2026-09-20: the swan nest, Canada Goose as a regular species, the unidentified diving duck as a real sighting, and the gulls in flight.

#### What this backfill cannot tell you

- **A third of the lake is missing.** S1 has never been photographed; S2 has two visits, both birdless. Every proportion below is an S3–S5 proportion. The gap is the 363 m from −74.00119 east to Ocean Avenue, and it contains two of the three crossings.
- **Counts are lower bounds.** Maximum-simultaneous reads, not censuses. A low count usually means birds were screened or out of frame, not absent — the brood reads 1 on 2026-09-14 and 4 the next week.
- **Sightings, not individuals.** One resident swan pair accounts for most of the Mute Swan total.
- **Effort is uneven.** Eleven of 30 dates are a single stop of 1–5 photos. The species mix describes where Geno walked as much as what lives here.
- **The detector is useless on ice.** On 2026-01-31 it returned 179 detections at >=0.30 and essentially every one was a lump of snow, scoring up to 0.78. That date was counted by eye.

### Entry 9 — exhaustive water-quality sweep of the whole archive, logged 2026-09-21

Not a bird entry. All 262 photographs swept frame by frame, cropped to the water
band, looking for visible pollutants. Recorded under PROTOCOL.md §3a in
`analysis/<date>-water.json`.

**An earlier claim in this project that the lake "photographs clean" was wrong.**
It rested on ~80 frames sampled from montages built for bird work — a set biased
toward frames with birds in them. The sweep found six things it had missed.

- **2026-03-25** — a **dumped drum**, yellow and green, roughly half a metre,
  half-submerged at the water's edge. The largest piece of dumped material in the
  archive. Whether it was ever removed is unknown.
- **2026-07-09** — water **strongly ochre-brown**, whole frame, under a clear sky.
- **2026-08-11** — a **floating carcass**, ~40 cm, across four frames. A waterbird
  or a large fish; not resolvable which. **Not a swan-family member** — two adults
  and all four cygnets were counted alive in the same event.
- **2026-08-12** — **vivid green floating patches**, clumped and drifting. Most
  consistent with duckweed or filamentous algae, but uncertain at that range. The
  only algae-like observation anywhere in the record, and small.
- **2026-09-17** — a **dead fish** on the dock planking, beside the cormorant.
  This independently **confirms Entry 2**, which recorded it from EXIF-stripped
  photographs. A written observation verified against the original.
- **2026-09-21** — litter and an organic debris mat at the western trash racks.

Water ran strongly ochre-brown on 07-09, 08-15, 09-04, 09-10 and 09-20; grey or
blue on 08-12, 01-31 and 09-21.

**None of this is a pollution event.** A single carcass is not a fish kill, and
§8's water-health signals are not met. What it is: a baseline, honestly taken,
across eight observations on seven dates. No statement about change over time is
available yet.

**A method rule was overturned in the process.** Colour had been excluded from
§3a outright, on pixel sampling that showed illumination dominating. Wrong test:
under a clear blue sky a merely reflective surface reads blue, so water reading
ochre-brown under blue sky is genuinely brown. Colour is now a judged category
gated on clear sky — never a measurement. Cause stays undeterminable from a
photograph; sediment, tannin and an algal tint all look alike.
