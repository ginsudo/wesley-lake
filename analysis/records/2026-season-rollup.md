## Method and its limits

Every date was swept by the YOLOX detector, grouped into sighting events by `framing.py`, and counted **per event at maximum simultaneous**, never summed across frames. Counts are lower bounds on birds present and say nothing about individuals: the same swan appears on 20 dates below and is counted 20 times.

Core count = birds on the lake surface (PROTOCOL.md §5b). Season total **143–170 bird-sightings** over 27 dates.

Open water dominates overwhelmingly, but that is partly an artefact: a bird on a mat is only assignable to a *named* mat when the mat is identifiable, and only MAT-01 has a durable mark. MAT-02 and MAT-03 hold one recorded sighting between them and were both photographed empty more often than occupied. The mats are the lake’s main roost structure (geography.md) and this table does not yet show that — it is measuring the registry’s weakness, not the birds’ behaviour.

**The brood of four survived the whole season.** Four on 2026-06-13 as downy young, four on 2026-08-12 in the cleanest single-frame count in the record, and four again on 2026-09-19. Every lower number is a partial view — birds screened by planting, strung across a channel, or outside the frame.

## Other findings

- **Canada Goose is a real and substantial part of this lake’s avifauna** — 36 sightings on five dates, second only to Mute Swan, and peaking at 10–12 on 2026-07-09 and 2026-09-17. The log previously held one goose record: Entry 3, "geese today", no EXIF, no count.
- **An unidentified diving duck on 2026-03-26** — dark head and breast, sharply demarcated white flanks, not a mallard. At ~53×30 px it is not resolvable and PROTOCOL.md §9 says to leave it there. Would be a first for the log.
- **Two swan behaviours new to the record**: hauling out onto mown grass (2026-06-19) and upending to feed (2026-09-14).
- **The best hard-mark check in the log**: the cormorant on the dock on 2026-09-17, feet fully resolved on bare planking — cleanly unbanded. A real negative, not a "not visible".
- **2026-09-13 is a genuine no-bird lake observation** — one photo, flat calm, whole lake in view, nothing on it.

## What this rollup is not

Sightings, not individuals. One resident swan pair accounts for most of the 44 Mute Swan sightings. Species proportions here describe **how often Geno saw something**, which is a function of where he walked and when, not of what lives on the lake. Eleven of the 27 dates are a single stop of 1–5 photos.
